# Email Templates — Shape + Voice Rules

Three emails. In order.

Every run outputs all three, filled in for the flagship target from phase 2 + the sneak peek angle from phase 3.

The prospect only sees emails 2 and 3 AFTER replying to email 1. So the whole sequence is triggered by a single "yes".

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)

- **No em-dashes.** Replace with a full stop, a comma, or a colon.
- **No exclamation marks.** Ever.
- **en-GB spelling.** "mocked up" not "mocked-up"; "colour" not "color"; "specialise" not "specialize".
- **Third-grade reading level.** Banned words: leverage, synergy, scale (as verb), elevate, unlock, streamline, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, next-gen, transform, empower, disrupt.
- **Literal outcomes over abstract states.** "Calls stop getting booked" beats "pipeline stalls". "Every store gets the wrong sign" beats "execution misalignment".
- **Short lines.** Most prospects read on mobile. Two-line paragraphs max.
- **One question per email.** Two questions splits attention and kills reply rates.
- **No pitch in email 1.** The whole job of email 1 is the yes. Nothing else.

## Client jargon rule

Some clients' whole brand equity is their proprietary language (WBD "Bowtie" / "SPICED", Dunford "positioning", specific methodology names).

Rule:

- **Zero jargon in the magnet name.** The magnet name must pass the 3-second stranger test. "Bowtie Preview" fails. "Revenue Motion Preview" passes.
- **Zero jargon in email 1.** The stranger reading email 1 does not know the term. Use plain English.
- **ONE piece of client jargon allowed in email 2.** The prospect has said yes, they've seen the image, they're warmer. One term reinforces differentiation: "This is a preview of the Bowtie we'd map for you."
- **Freely use jargon in email 3.** By this point they've asked for the call, they're bought in.

## Email 1 — Initial "want this?" outbound

**Subject shape:** lowercase, 4-6 words, mention the target company name. No colons. No "quick question" openings.

Examples:
- `b&m spring garden preview`
- `mocked up a POS preview for [target]`
- `a preview we put together for [target]`

**Body shape (4-6 lines total):**

Line 1: One-line observation grounded in something specific about the target (their scale, their store count, their seasonal cycle). Not a compliment. An observation.

Line 2-3: What we made. Name the deliverable. Be specific about what it shows.

Line 4: The question. "Worth sending over?" / "Want me to send it?" / "OK if I send it?"

**Never in email 1:** the offer, the client company name, the workflow chain, the price, any book-a-call ask.

## Email 2 — Sneak peek reply

Fires when the prospect replies yes to email 1.

**Attachment / inline image:** the CROPPED preview. Not the full board. A slice that shows enough to create curiosity and hides enough to force the call.

**Body shape (5-8 lines):**

Line 1: Acknowledge the yes in one word. "Great." / "Nice." / "Ok."

Line 2-3: Frame what they're looking at. Name the deliverable properly this time. Point at 2-3 things visible in the preview so their eye knows where to land.

Line 4-5: Say what the FULL version has that the preview doesn't. Be specific. "The full version has the rollout structure, production notes, and store-kit breakdown." This is the curiosity gap.

Line 6: The book-a-call ask. Direct. "Worth me walking you through it properly this week?"

**Never in email 2:** attaching the full deliverable, sending a booking link (the prospect proposes the time or asks how, not the other way round).

## Email 3 — Book-a-call reply

Fires when the prospect asks about the call, asks for the full report, or says yes to a meeting.

**Body shape (5-7 lines):**

Line 1: Confirm the meeting or propose a time bracket ("Tuesday morning or Wednesday afternoon UK?").

Line 2-3: Preview what happens on the call. "I'll walk you through the full board, the rollout plan for [target], and what production and install would actually look like across your [N] stores."

Line 4: Set expectations on length. "Should take about 20 minutes."

Line 5: A calendar link OR a request for their availability. Whichever the operator prefers.

**Never in email 3:** rebuilding the pitch, restating the value prop, apologising for the delay.

## Filled example — BPD → B&M

### Email 1

**Subject:** b&m spring garden preview

Saw how much seasonal retail volume B&M runs across home, garden, toys, food, and promo.

We mocked up a quick branded in-store campaign preview showing how a spring garden promo could look across windows, aisle displays, shelf POS, and dump bins.

Worth sending over?

### Email 2

Nice.

Here is the sneak preview. You can see how the campaign could work as a full store rollout across store entry, aisle, shelf, and promo display formats. Not just isolated print assets.

The full version has the rollout structure, production notes, and store-kit breakdown for your 777 stores.

Worth walking you through it properly this week?

### Email 3

Happy to. Tuesday morning or Wednesday afternoon UK.

I will walk you through the full board, the rollout plan for B&M, and what production and install would actually look like across your 777 stores. Including the campaign kit box format so store teams get a plug-and-play install.

Should take about 20 minutes.

Here is my calendar: [link]

## What to check before shipping any run's emails

- Every em-dash removed
- Every exclamation mark removed
- Every banned word from the list above removed
- Every abstract state converted to a literal event
- Every email has exactly one question
- Email 1 mentions the target's real scale, not a generic compliment
- Email 2 names 2-3 specific things visible in the preview
- Email 3 sets the meeting length

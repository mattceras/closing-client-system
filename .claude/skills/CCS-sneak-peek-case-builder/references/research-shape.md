# Research Memo — Required Shape

The research memo is the "What this client actually does" section (message 1, section 1) of every skill run.

Every section is one paragraph plus (where relevant) a short bullet list. Never write essays. Use the EXACT plain-English labels below.

## Client type prefix

Before writing the memo, decide which of these two shapes the client is:

- **Company** — has employees, price tiers, a client roster. BPD, Kalungi, Winning by Design, Leads4You.
- **Personal brand / solo operator** — an individual selling their own expertise. Author-consultants, coaches, fractional executives, keynote speakers. April Dunford, a solo fractional CFO, a founder coach.

If **personal brand**, use the section swaps noted below (marked with 👤 PB).

---

## 1. What they really sell (not what the site says)

Not what they call themselves. What they commercially DO.

The wedge that makes them worth more than a commodity.

## 2. What they can actually deliver

Concrete services + infrastructure (tech, tools, machinery) visible on-site.

Group by function. Depends on the business type:
- **Service business** — creative / production / delivery / support
- **Software / SaaS** — core product / integrations / support / infrastructure
- **Agency** — strategy / execution / reporting / account management
- **Community / info product** — community / content / events / resources
- 👤 **PB — Personal brand** — replace this section with **"What they personally do vs what they distribute for free"**. The paid engagement is a small list (a workshop, a retainer, a coaching arc). The free distribution is separate (books, podcast, keynotes) and belongs top-of-funnel, not in what-they-deliver.

Purpose: operator should be able to point at capability #3 during the sales conversation and say "this is the piece that unlocks the story".

## 3. Who they've already worked with

Named clients + what each proves. Rank by strength.

If the client has no named clients on-site (common for younger businesses), note what CATEGORY of client they've worked with instead. Do not invent names.

👤 **PB — Personal brand:** replace with **"Named endorsements or platform quotes"** — the equivalent proof for a personal brand is famous people who publicly credit them, not case study clients. e.g. "Postman CEO called her the GOAT of positioning" is stronger than a client list.

**Logo-conflict warning:** the flagship target picked in phase 3 MUST NOT already appear on this list. Cross-check before picking. If your candidate is already a client, pick the next one down.

## 4. Price band + sales motion

Two lines only:
- **Price band** — one-off cost / monthly subscription range / typical deal size (extract from site or estimate from category)
- **Sales motion** — self-serve checkout, sales call required, or contract-and-onboard

This section is new and important. It determines whether the sneak peek mechanism even applies (see phase 2 of SKILL.md).

## 5. Company history flags (skip if clean)

Any recent administration, rebranding, restructure, or ownership change.

Skip this section if history is clean.

👤 **PB — Personal brand:** replace with **"Reputation signal"** — what public assets built their brand (books, podcasts, keynotes, notable quotes). This tells the operator what NOT to compete with in the cold email (don't try to teach them a concept they're already famous for).

## 6. Who their buyer actually is

The specific ICP the CLIENT sells to. Named titles inside the buying company.

For each role, one line on their actual pain (what wakes them up).

Group by ICP type if the client serves multiple:
- Multi-site retailer / consumer brand / agency / procurement / founder-buyer / etc.

**Hard rule for unclear ICP:** if the site does not name an industry, size, and buyer role, write EXACTLY ONE sentence: **"ICP not extractable from site."** Then stop. Do NOT list possibilities ("could be X, Y, or Z") — that language is how the skill accidentally locks in a persona and builds magnets against a guess. Triggering Outcome B from phase 2 is the correct path when ICP is unclear.

## 7. Where their own marketing misses the buyer

What their site actually says vs. what a buyer actually thinks.

The gap is where the operator's outbound wins.

## 8. Fit for cold outbound?

One paragraph. Blunt answer. Which of the three phase-2 outcomes does this client hit?

**Standard fit, per-prospect** — client sells to specific named enterprise buyers who take discovery calls. One image per prospect. BPD, Sopro, SwagUp, Belkins.

**Standard fit, generic** — client sells to a repeatable ICP where one mock-up works across the whole list. Leads4You, Kalungi, a lead-gen agency serving founders.

**Fit with risk** — the product doesn't match the standard cold-outbound shape (low-ticket self-serve, mass ICP, personal brand on inbound, B2C). The skill still produces the playbook but flags the risk at the top of Message 1 and suggests what to adjust. Todoist (target enterprise team plan not individual), a solo consultant with an inbound-only brand (send from operator on their team), etc.

**ICP unclear** — the site does not name industry, size, or buyer role. This is an information gap. Skill stops phase 2 and asks the operator to name the ICP before proposing magnets.

---

## Length target

400-700 words total. If a section has nothing to say, one line explaining why. Never delete a section header.

# Cold Traffic Offer Creation

Framework for evaluating, reframing, and constructing offers that work in cold email. Run this before writing any copy. Most bad cold email results trace back to a warm offer being forced into a cold channel — not bad copy.

---

## The Core Distinction

**Warm offer:** Only converts when the prospect already trusts you, already understands the problem, and already believes you can solve it. Works in referral, inbound, and relationship-driven sales. Dies in cold inboxes.

**Cold traffic offer:** Works on a stranger. The prospect can say yes without:
- Trusting you
- Understanding how your system or mechanism works
- Believing you're any good at what you do
- Being emotionally ready to commit to a full engagement

If the offer requires any of those four things to convert, it's a warm offer and needs to be reframed before it goes into a cold campaign.

---

## The Four Tests

Run every client's offer through these four tests. If it fails any of them, it needs a reframe.

### Test 1 — Specific Numeric Outcome
There is a real number attached to the result. Not "more leads" — "8-12 qualified meetings per month." Not "grow revenue" — "add $100K in net margin in 5 months." The number doesn't have to be guaranteed — it can be a ballpark, a range, or anchored to a case study. But it has to exist.

### Test 2 — Finite Timeframe and Scope
The offer has a defined end. "90-day pipeline accelerator." "First 60 days." "By end of Q2." Open-ended offers ("ongoing partnership," "retainer engagement," "we'll be your growth team") fail because the prospect can't see the bottom of the commitment.

### Test 3 — New Money or Opportunity, Not Optimization
The offer produces something that doesn't exist yet — new channel, new revenue, new pipeline, new margin the business isn't currently capturing. Optimization offers ("we'll make your existing X better") fail because they require the prospect to already believe there's a problem worth fixing, and to believe you're the expert who should fix it.

This is the test most clients fail. It requires the most reframing work — see The Reframe Move below.

### Test 4 — Safe: Low Trust Required to Say Yes
The downside of saying yes is near zero. The offer structure has to be low-stakes enough that a stranger can agree to the next step without needing to trust you yet. Pilots, proof-of-concepts, short paid tests, and free deliverables all qualify. A 12-month retainer does not.

## The Language Tell

The fastest way to spot a warm offer is by the verbs.

**Cold offer verbs (generative, net-new):**
Generate, build, get, land, book, source, add, bring in, produce, deliver, create, win, close

These verbs describe things that don't exist yet. They promise new output.

**Warm offer verbs (optimization, management, improvement):**
Optimize, improve, manage, streamline, enhance, refine, scale, grow, support, partner, help with, handle, take over

These verbs describe acting on something that already exists. They assume the prospect already believes there's something worth acting on.

**Quick test:** Read the client's current positioning out loud. If the main verbs are from the second list, the offer needs a reframe.

## The Reframe Move: Optimization Services → Cold Offers

Most B2B services are inherently optimization work — MSPs, fractional CFOs, RevOps consultants, ops consultants. The actual service is "we make your existing operation better." That's optimization. That's warm.

**The reframe:** Don't change what the service does. Write the offer to the end business outcome, not to the service itself.

> The end business outcome of almost every B2B service is more money, more margin, more pipeline, or more time. Write the offer to that outcome. Describe the service in the second line as the mechanism — never as the offer itself.

### The template

> **Line 1:** End business outcome with a number and a timeframe.
> **Line 2:** The mechanism — "by doing X, Y, Z."

Line 1 is the offer. Line 2 is the service. Clients almost always write the service as the offer and wonder why it doesn't land.

### Worked example — cost recovery consultant for general contractors

**Bad (warm) — describes the service:**
"Eliminate guesswork in estimating overhead."
→ Requires the prospect to already believe they have a guesswork problem. Three warm-audience assumptions.

**Still bad (warm) — describes the problem:**
"General contractors lose $10K-$50K a year in overages, insurance gaps, and downtime between jobs."
→ Closer, but still warm. Asks the prospect to accept you as the expert on their problems.

**Cold — describes the end business outcome:**
"Add $100K of net margin in 5 months. You don't pay unless we hit it."
→ The prospect doesn't need to know what the service is. They just need to want $100K in net margin. The mechanism goes in line 2: "By auditing your overhead, insurance coverage, and crew comp structure to surface recoverable margin."

### Reframe table by service type

| Service | Warm framing (the service) | Cold framing (the outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| MSP / managed IT | "We'll manage your IT infrastructure" | "Cut $30K-$80K in wasted software licenses and downtime costs in 90 days" |
| Fractional CFO | "We'll clean up your financials" | "Find $50K-$200K in recoverable margin in 60 days" |
| AI operations | "We'll embed AI into your operations" | "Add 15-25% margin back by eliminating [specific manual process] in 90 days" |
| RevOps consulting | "We'll optimize your sales process" | "Close 20-30% more of your existing pipeline in 90 days without adding headcount" |
| Cost recovery / audit | "We'll audit your expenses" | "Recover $X-$Y in overpayments in 60 days — you don't pay unless we find it" |
| Route optimization | "We'll optimize your delivery routes" | "Cut 15-20% of fuel and driver costs in 30 days without changing your fleet" |
| Cold email agency | "We'll manage your outbound" | "Generate 8-12 qualified sales meetings per month in 90 days" |

Every right column leads with a number, a timeframe, and a net-new outcome. Every left column describes a service.

## Wedge Offers: The Front-End / Back-End Structure

Most clients instinctively try to sell their entire suite through cold email. This almost never works.

**The cold offer's job is to get the meeting, not to sell the full engagement.**

### The wedge offer (what goes in the email)
- A pilot
- A proof of concept
- First 90 days with a specific deliverable
- A paid audit or diagnostic
- A lead magnet or one-pager that leads to a call
- A short, finite engagement with a specific numeric outcome

### The back-end offer (what gets sold on the call)
- The 12-month retainer
- The full-service package
- The ongoing relationship
- The multi-phase engagement

### AVAERO's own structure as example
- **Wedge:** 90-Day Pipeline Accelerator — specific outcome, finite timeframe, scoped deliverable
- **Back-end:** Ongoing retainer with list building, enrichment, sequencing, SDR calling, CRM management, strategy

The wedge is what goes in the email. The back-end is what gets sold after the prospect is warm.

**When onboarding a new client, always ask: "What's your wedge?"**
If they don't have one, help them build one before writing any copy. A client without a wedge is not a cold email client — they're a referral business that needs to either develop a wedge offer or run a different channel.

## Risk Reversal

Risk reversal lives on a ladder. You don't always need to mention it in the email — but the offer structure has to be low-stakes enough that a stranger can say yes.

**The ladder (strongest to weakest):**
1. **Pay on performance** — "You don't pay unless we hit $100K in margin."
2. **Money-back guarantee** — "If we don't deliver X in Y days, full refund."
3. **Free pilot / free trial** — "Free 30-day pilot. If it works, we'll talk about next steps."
4. **Paid pilot with exit ramp** — "90-day engagement. No obligation to continue."
5. **Free deliverable upfront** — "We'll build you a custom list of 100 targeted contacts just for hopping on the call."
6. **"Even if you don't work with us"** — "I'll break down the strategy even if you just want to take it and run with it yourself."
7. **Case study match** — "We just did this for a similar [business type]." (Social proof as implicit risk reversal.)

AVAERO's Pipeline Accelerator stacks #4 + #5 + #6 together.

**Put risk reversal in the email copy when:**
- The service is expensive or high-commitment (makes the ask feel smaller)
- The vertical is skeptical of the client's service category (cold email, AI, consulting)
- The client has a strong case study match

**Leave it out when:**
- The offer is already obviously low-risk (free deliverable, short pilot)
- Including it would make the email too long
- The number itself is doing the heavy lifting

## Anti-Patterns: Warm Offer Language to Flag

When you see these on a client's website, in their onboarding form, or in draft copy — flag them and push for a reframe.

**Vague partnership language:**
- "We'll be your growth partner"
- "Let us be an extension of your team"
- "We help companies scale"
- "We partner with [vertical] to [verb]"

**Optimization language:**
- "We'll optimize your [X]"
- "We'll streamline your [X]"
- "We'll improve your [X]"

**Expertise-assumption language:**
- "Leverage our expertise in [X]"
- "With 20 years of experience, we..."
- "Our proprietary [X] framework"

**Open-ended CTAs:**
- "Book a strategy call to learn more"
- "Let's chat about how we can help"
- "Schedule a discovery call"

**Process language:**
- "We'll handle everything from X to Y to Z"
- "Full-service [X]"
- "End-to-end [X]"
- "Done-for-you [X]" *(borderline — works if paired with a specific outcome, fails if it's the whole pitch)*

## Diagnostic Checklist

Run this before touching any copy. If the offer fails two or more tests, stop and reframe first.

**The Four Tests**
- [ ] Is there a specific number in the outcome?
- [ ] Is there a finite timeframe and scope?
- [ ] Does the offer produce new money or new opportunity — not optimize existing operations?
- [ ] Can a stranger say yes to the next step without trusting the sender?

**The Language Tell**
- [ ] Are the main verbs generative (generate, build, get, land, source, add)?
- [ ] Is the offer written to the end business outcome, not the service itself?

**The Structure**
- [ ] Is there a clear wedge offer separate from the back-end engagement?
- [ ] Is the wedge what's going in the email — not the full suite of services?

**The Safety**
- [ ] Is the offer low-stakes enough that a stranger can say yes to the next step?
- [ ] If the ask is high-stakes, is there risk reversal bolted on?

**If the offer fails two or more tests:** stop and reframe before writing copy.

**If the offer can't be reframed:** this client may not be a cold email client. Sometimes the right move is to tell them the truth and not take the work.

## Optional: Score It (4 Pillars)

The Diagnostic Checklist above is a pass/fail read. Use that by default. Switch to this numeric version when the user specifically asks for a score, a percentage, a rating, or "what's the probability this works on cold" — it's a more granular, calibrated version of the same underlying tests.

Score the offer on four dials, 1-4 each (half-points allowed), sum to a total out of 16:

- **New Money** — the graded version of Test 3 above (net-new revenue vs. optimizing existing assets).
- **Done-For-You** — a dimension not covered elsewhere in this skill: does the prospect perceive they don't have to lift a finger? A course or coaching program where the student does the work caps low here even if it passes every other test — this is often *why* a coaching program that looks cold-shaped on paper still doesn't convert on cold traffic.
- **Derisked** — the graded version of the Risk Reversal ladder above (guarantee strength, performance pricing, case study quality).
- **Low Commitment** — the graded version of Test 4 above, but category-relative: the same dollar amount scores differently for a book vs. a DFY sprint vs. a recurring retainer vs. a high-ticket mastermind, because the prospect anchors against different comparables.

Convert the total to a rough percentage (13/16 ≈ 81%) and a verdict: 14-16 works on cold as-is, 11-13 works with optimization, 8-10 borderline/needs rework, below 8 this is a warm traffic offer.

## The One-Line Summary

> A cold traffic offer promises net-new money or opportunity, in a specific amount, in a finite window, to a stranger who doesn't have to trust you to say yes. Write the offer to the end business outcome. Put the service in the second line as the mechanism.

# Numeric Scoring Rubric — 4 Pillars

Full tier-by-tier detail for the optional scoring method described in the main SKILL.md. Use this when the user wants a specific score, percentage, or grade rather than just a pass/fail read from the Diagnostic Checklist.

Score each dial independently on a 1-4 scale. Half-points allowed (1.5, 2.5, 3.5). Be conservative — don't inflate.

---

## Pillar 1: New Money

Same underlying concept as Test 3 in the main skill (New Money or Opportunity, Not Optimization) — this just grades it instead of pass/failing it.

**4/4 — Clean new money.** New leads, new customers, or new revenue from sources the prospect wasn't tapping before. No ambiguity.

**3/4 — Net-new outcome with some mechanism ambiguity.** The outcome is net-new, but the mechanism is one step removed from direct revenue (authority building, positioning), or there's some "scalping" flavor in the framing even though the underlying thing is new.

**2/4 — Scalping existing assets but creating some net-new components.** The core is optimizing/extracting from what the prospect already has, but new things are also being built in the process (e.g. new email flows built, but applied to an existing list).

**1/4 — Pure optimization/reactivation.** Entirely about squeezing more out of existing assets with no net-new creation.

**Word trickery doesn't bump the score.** Positioning reactivation as "booked appointments" works as marketing but the underlying mechanism still scores 2/4 for scoring purposes, regardless of how it's framed on the landing page. Vague deliverables also cap this at 2 — no clear thing being generated means no clean New Money claim.

## Pillar 2: Done-For-You

**Not covered elsewhere in this skill — this is the one genuinely new dimension the scoring system adds.** Measures whether the prospect perceives they don't have to lift a finger, separate from whether the offer is otherwise cold-shaped.

**4/4 — Real humans doing real work, client does nothing.** DFY cold email sending, DFY cold calling, DFY ad management where the agency runs everything.

**3/4 — Hybrid with a real DFY element appended, or full DFY requiring meaningful client integration.** A coaching program with a genuine DFY component bolted on ("we'll build your funnel AND teach you to run it") scores 3 if the DFY element is real, not just advertised. Also covers full-DFY offers needing heavy client access/decisions (fractional-style offers).

**2/4 — Done-With-You.** Provider does some of the work (copy, pages, templates) but the client implements. "We'll give you templates plus guidance" lands here.

**1/4 — Client does all the work.** Course, playbook, book, self-paced program.

**Be honest, not generous.** A program that markets itself as "done for you" but actually requires student implementation is 1-2/4 regardless of marketing. This is also why "Done-for-you [X]" is flagged as borderline in the Anti-Patterns section above — the word alone doesn't make it true.

## Pillar 3: Derisked

Same underlying concept as the Risk Reversal ladder above — this grades where an offer lands on that ladder instead of just listing the options.

**4/4 — Pure performance pricing, OR money-back outcome guarantee + quantified case studies.** "$0 management fee, pay $X per booked appointment" with case studies showing real client revenue. Or a real money-back guarantee tied to a specific outcome, plus specific ROI case studies.

**3/4 — Outcome-based guarantee OR strong quantified case studies with ROI.** One of: a meaningful outcome guarantee (even if not full money-back); or 5+ case studies with quantified ROI. Not pure performance, not household-name proof.

**2/4 — Performance component with a fixed base, OR decent case studies without big names, OR general testimonials with some results.** E.g. "$2.5k/mo + 10% rev share" (still has fixed out-of-pocket).

**1/4 — No real derisking.** No guarantee, or only a "delivery guarantee" (worth zero — not scamming people is table stakes, not derisking). No meaningful case studies.

**Guarantee proximity to money matters:** clients signed > calls booked > leads > activity metrics. The closer the guarantee is to actual money, the higher the score and the more can be charged. Money-back beats free-extended-service ("if it didn't work, why would they want more of it?"). Contract lock-in hurts this dial too, not just Low Commitment.

## Pillar 4: Low Commitment

Same underlying concept as Test 4 (Safe) above, but category-relative rather than a single bar. The same dollar amount scores differently depending on what the prospect is mentally comparing it to.

**Books / playbooks / digital downloads:** anchor against $10-20 Kindle books and $30 info products.
- 4/4: under $30 · 3/4: $30-50 · 2/4: $50-150 · 1/4: $200+

**Courses (self-paced or light live component):** anchor against $49 Udemy courses and $197 info products.
- 4/4: under $50 · 3/4: $50-200 · 2/4: $200-400 · 1.5/4: $497-700 · 1/4: $1,000+

**DFY services / sprints (one-time payment):** anchor against $3k+ monthly retainers.
- 4/4: under $2,000 · 3/4: $2,000-4,000 · 2.5/4: $4,000-5,000 · 2/4: $5,000-10,000 · 1/4: $10,000+

**Recurring services:** prospects mentally multiply by 12 and feel the lock-in.
- 3/4: under $500/mo, no contract · 2/4: $500-2,000/mo, no contract · 1/4: $2,000+/mo OR any multi-month contract

**High-ticket coaching/mastermind:**
- 2/4: under $5,000 one-time or short program · 1/4: $5,000+ one-time or any meaningful recurring commitment
- These almost always land at 1/4 on cold — they're structurally warm traffic offers.

**Modifiers:**
- Any 3-month+ minimum on a recurring offer caps this at 1/4, full stop.
- An ad-spend requirement on top of the fee structurally caps this at 2.5/4 even with a clean one-time payment and no contract.
- "One-time payment, no retainers, no contracts" language in the headline measurably helps perceived commitment, even at the same dollar amount.
- A trial structure ("first month $500, then $2,500/mo") can push this up a full point vs. full-price recurring from day one — same idea as the wedge/back-end split above, applied to pricing specifically.

## Scoring rules

1. Score each dial independently — don't let one influence another at the scoring layer. Compensation happens at the total, not per-dial.
2. Use half-points liberally; most offers don't land cleanly on integers.
3. Sum to a total out of 16. Convert to a rough percentage (13/16 ≈ 81%) — this is a vibes-based estimate, not a statistical claim.
4. Verdict thresholds: **14-16** works on cold as-is · **11-13** works with optimization · **8-10** borderline, needs significant rework or is a warm offer · **below 8** this is a warm traffic offer, don't run ads/cold email to it as-is.

## Output format for a scored analysis

```
## Cold Traffic Offer Score

**Overall: [X]/16 — [Probability %]**
**Verdict: [Works on cold as-is / Works with optimization / Needs significant rework / This is a warm traffic offer]**

[One direct paragraph tying the verdict to their specific offer.]

### New Money: [X]/4
[2-3 sentences of reasoning tied to their offer specifically.]
**To push this higher:** [specific rewrite, not generic advice]

### Done-For-You: [X]/4
[reasoning]
**To push this higher:** [specific — name the exact element to add or reposition]

### Derisked: [X]/4
[reasoning, referencing their actual guarantee/case studies]
**To push this higher:** [rewrite their guarantee or propose a specific pricing structure]

### Low Commitment: [X]/4
[reasoning, naming which category tier applies to them]
**To push this higher:** [specific price/structure alternative]

---
### The Highest-Leverage Change
[ONE thing to fix first — not a list of ten.]
```

Formatting: no em dashes in the scored output (hyphens, semicolons, or new sentences instead). Be direct — "this is a warm traffic offer" rather than "this has some challenges for cold traffic." Quote back the user's own language so the analysis feels specific to them, not canned.

# CCS Proficiency Model

Use this only when updating `coaching-progress.md`, changing coaching depth, or assessing readiness for independent operation.

## Status levels

Assess each relevant capability separately:

- **Not started:** no meaningful evidence yet.
- **Guided:** completed with step-by-step direction; the AI or coach made most decisions.
- **Practicing:** can explain the decision rule and make part of the decision, but still needs correction or confirmation.
- **Independent:** can complete and explain the routine workflow without step-by-step help and catches common errors.
- **Proven:** has done this independently across at least two real situations or has one sustained result cycle with documented learning.

Do not infer competence from file ownership, tool access, confidence, or a single successful AI-generated output.

## Capability areas

Only score areas that matter to the student's business:

1. **Offer and wedge:** distinguishes warm from cold offers, defines a safe finite next step, and does not invent proof.
2. **ICP and market selection:** chooses a reachable market with a defensible reason for the segment.
3. **Lists and signals:** chooses company-first sources, understands what a signal actually proves, and pilots before scaling.
4. **Data and enrichment:** checks coverage and quality, uses tools economically, and avoids treating enrichment guesses as facts.
5. **Copy and sequences:** connects offer, market, proof, and CTA; writes concise variants with a testable hypothesis.
6. **Launch judgment:** verifies targeting, data, copy, sending setup, and approvals before activation.
7. **Campaign diagnosis:** uses sent volume, human and positive replies, meetings, and sample size rather than opens or intuition alone.
8. **Experiment design:** preserves a control, changes one strategic variable, and records wins and failed tests.
9. **Reply handling:** identifies fit and intent, moves qualified prospects toward a meeting, and knows when not to respond.
10. **Sales follow-through:** prepares for calls, records decisions, follows up, and keeps commercial terms accurate.
11. **System operation:** knows what can run locally, what needs cloud setup, where private data lives, and when approval is required.

## Coaching-level rules

The overall coaching level is about the student's normal operating scope, not the least-used CCS feature.

### Guided

Use when the core workflow is new, the student cannot yet identify the next stage, or major choices are routinely made for them.

### Collaborative

Use when the student understands the workflow and can critique outputs but still benefits from recommendations at key decision points.

### On demand

Use when the student independently runs routine campaigns and mainly needs analysis, quality control, unusual research, or automation.

### Graduated

Recommend graduated peer-review mode when there is evidence that the student can:

- independently move a suitable offer through targeting, data, copy, launch, and measurement;
- diagnose the current bottleneck from actual results;
- run a controlled iteration without destroying the control;
- handle common interested replies and meeting movement;
- explain the safety boundaries and when outside expertise is required.

The student does not need to master every integration or advanced feature. Graduation is valid for a defined scope, such as “independent for PlusVibe cold-email campaigns; still guided for cloud automation.”

## Evidence entries

Keep entries concise and specific:

```markdown
- 2026-09-01 — Campaign diagnosis — Practicing — Correctly identified low positive-reply rate as the constraint after reviewing adequate send volume; needed help separating offer and list hypotheses.
```

Record what the student demonstrated, the level supported, and any remaining gap. Do not store credentials, raw prospect data, or sensitive client exports in the progress file.

## Graduation conversation

When graduation criteria are met:

1. Say plainly that routine CCS work no longer requires guided coaching.
2. Name the exact scope and the evidence.
3. Switch future responses to concise peer-review mode.
4. Offer periodic audits, experiment reviews, and support for unusual cases.
5. Remind the student that asking for help later does not reverse graduation.

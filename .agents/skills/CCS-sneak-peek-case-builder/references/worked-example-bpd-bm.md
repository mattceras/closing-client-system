# Worked Example — BPD Group → B&M (PER-PROSPECT magnet)

This is the example for CLIENTS whose lead magnet is **per-prospect** — one image per lead, because the mock-up needs the specific prospect's logo, brand colours, or context to land. Contrast with the Leads4You → Directive example, which is **generic**.

**Context:** BPD Group (bpd-group.co.uk) is a real client — retail POS execution business, sells to multi-site retailers, per-prospect campaign previews. B&M is the flagship dream-fit target the skill identified. The Sneak Peek image is what actually gets sent to B&M after they reply YES to email 1.

**Not the canonical template for every run.** Only use this shape when the client's product is per-prospect enterprise (BPD, Sopro, big lead-gen agencies with named enterprise buyers). For generic magnets see `worked-example-leads4you.md`.

Source: original ChatGPT thread run by Sean, 2026-07-01.

## What this client actually does

### What they really sell (not what the site says)

BPD Group is a retail-focused visual communications / POS execution business, not a generic print shop. Their commercial wedge is outsourced retail marketing / POS execution for brands and retailers where speed, cost-control, store consistency, and rollout logistics matter.

Their own positioning is stronger than "print". They sell retail campaign execution infrastructure. That matters because "printing" commoditises them. "Retail campaign rollout without supplier chaos" is where the money is.

### What they can actually deliver

- **Creative / graphic design.** In-house design team for retail-facing artwork, POS displays, brand-guideline alignment, large-format optimisation.
- **Digital print / large format production.** High-end kit including EFI Nozomi 14000 SD, HP11000/HP17000, Mimaki, Ricoh Latex, Esko Kongsberg CAD cutting tables, Young Shin die-cutting, GMG Colorserver, Chili Publish, Esko Webcenter, Store Visualiser.
- **3D design / CAD prototyping.** Technical drawings, dielines, prototypes, white samples, manufacturing specs before production. Commercially important because it reduces buyer risk before a full rollout commit.
- **Logistics / fulfilment / installation.** Warehousing, delivery partners, multi-site rollout, delivery tracking. Their own words: "warehousing to multi-site rollouts".
- **Sustainability.** Recycled / recyclable / FSC substrates, efficient production, local suppliers, reduced waste. Support angle only, not the primary wedge unless selling into ESG-heavy enterprise buyers.

### Who they've already worked with

- **One Stop — 10-year partnership.** Tesco-owned convenience retailer, 1,000+ shops, 10,600+ colleagues. Strongest commercial proof. Longevity + big-retail credibility. Use line: "Trusted by Tesco-owned One Stop for 10 years."
- **Dobbies Garden Centres.** National garden centre chain, heritage to 1865. POS and signage for high-footfall zones. Best national-retail logo.
- **Candy Kittens.** UK confectionery, B Corp. Branded sampling stall in a London train station. Strongest FMCG / brand-activation proof.
- **KENJI.** UK lifestyle retailer, 201-500 employees. Bespoke retail display stall. Useful but lighter than the top three.

### Company history flags

Serious wrinkle. The current trading entity is B-Print, Display & Logistics Limited, incorporated 5 January 2026, active, SIC 18129. The older company, B.Print & Display Limited (incorporated 1981), is in Administration as of 24 March 2026. FRP Advisory sold the business and assets out of administration, preserving 41 jobs.

Blunt read: this is a reset business with real capability, real legacy, and pressure to turn new infrastructure into revenue fast. Do not lead outbound with "family-run since 1981" unless the client is comfortable with how the newco / administration context is handled.

### Who inside a target company would say yes

**Multi-site retailers.** Head of Retail Marketing, Trade Marketing Manager, Visual Merchandising Manager, POS Manager, Procurement Manager, Store Development. Pain: inconsistent store execution, supplier sprawl, late campaign changes, poor print quality, hidden cost, missed rollout deadlines.

**CPG / FMCG / consumer brands.** Brand Manager, Trade Marketing Manager, Shopper Marketing Manager, Category Manager, National Account Manager. Pain: new SKU launches, seasonal campaigns, FSDUs, sampling stalls, in-store visibility, retailer compliance.

**Agencies serving retail brands.** Account Director, Production Director, Creative Director, Head of Delivery. Pain: win the creative but need reliable production, prototyping, logistics, and install capacity.

**Procurement-led enterprise buyers.** Procurement Manager, Print Buyer, Indirect Procurement, Marketing Procurement. Pain: too many vendors, markups, no single owner, inconsistent quality, uncontrolled spend.

### Ways to pitch them that will land

- **Retail print cost leakage.** "Find where your retail print/POS budget is leaking before your next campaign rollout." Speaks to procurement and finance pressure, not creative preference.
- **Multi-site rollout control.** "One accountable partner for artwork, production, fulfilment, and install. Every store gets the right assets, on time, without supplier chaos."
- **Speed-to-store.** "Turn campaign changes into store-ready assets faster, without sacrificing colour consistency or brand control." Leans on the Nozomi capacity story.
- **Prototype before rollout.** "See the display before committing thousands to production." Reduces buyer fear on physical POS, FSDUs, stalls, bespoke displays.
- **Supplier consolidation.** "Replace creative-production-logistics fragmentation with one retail POS execution partner." Cleanest enterprise procurement message.

### Where their own marketing misses the buyer

Their site and LinkedIn say things like "large format print", "high-impact visuals", "creative expertise", "seamless logistics", "sustainable print", "end-to-end solutions". Fine. Doesn't punch.

The buyer does not wake up thinking "I need large format print". They wake up thinking:

- "Our campaign assets are late again."
- "Stores are installing the wrong POS."
- "Procurement wants supplier rationalisation."
- "We have six vendors and nobody owns the outcome."
- "We need this launch live in 400 stores by Monday."
- "We are wasting budget on rework and overproduction."

That is where BPD's outbound should live.

### The offer you could build with them

Package BPD around commercial outcomes instead of print capabilities. Named offer: "Retail POS Spend & Rollout Audit". Promise: "We will show you where your retail POS campaign spend is leaking across suppliers, rework, artwork bottlenecks, fulfilment, and store execution."

Procurement cares. Marketing cares. Finance cares. Opens conversations without sounding like a printer chasing quote requests. Lets BPD sell the full-stack solution naturally.

## What could go wrong

- **BPD's admin history**: if the buyer diligences the trading entity and sees the March 2026 administration event, they may pause. Get ahead of it in the meeting, not in email 1.
- **Colour fidelity risk on the mock-up**: if the ChatGPT image has the wrong B&M orange or a mangled logo, the buyer will read it as "these people don't actually know retail". Have the operator eyeball the render before sending.
- **Wrong buyer contact**: if the initial email lands with a store manager instead of trade marketing / procurement, no meeting. Enrich the list carefully before sending.

# Signal-Led List Building — Public Directory Methodology

This methodology is included as part of the Closing Client System's list-building knowledge.

---

## How to Use This Bank

Do not start by asking, "Which list should I scrape?" Start by asking what proof of activity would make your message relevant. The directory is the source. The signal is the reason to write.

---

## Operating Model: Build Every Campaign from Signal to Relevance

A public directory is useful when it tells you something specific: a company won a contract, filed a permit, earned a certification, joined an association, paid for an exhibitor booth, registered as a vendor, or appeared in a regulated database. Once you understand what the signal implies, the list, contact pull, and opener become downstream execution.

### Five Steps

1. **Find the signal** — Filter the bank by source family and geography, then identify what action caused the company to appear.
2. **Read the implication** — Translate the directory fact into a business condition: expansion, compliance burden, procurement intent, hiring pressure, or active market entry.
3. **Map the problem** — Decide which operational, revenue, capital, hiring, or risk problem your offer can credibly solve.
4. **Pull the right people** — Scrape only the relevant company set, enrich for decision-makers, and remove titles that cannot act on the problem.
5. **Write around the proof** — Use the signal as the reason for the email, not as trivia. The opener should make the offer feel connected to what happened.

---

## Four Build Motions

### Direct Source Build
**Companies in the source ARE the prospects.**

Use when: the directory already contains your buyer universe. Pull manufacturers from Thomasnet, contractors from licence boards, healthcare operators from provider datasets, or vendors from procurement systems. Filter tightly before enrichment so the list is a market, not a dump.

### Reverse Build
**Companies in the source BECOME the hook.**

Use when: your buyer serves the companies listed in the directory. A list of funded contractors, certified suppliers, or regulated facilities can become a lead magnet for consultants, lenders, recruiters, insurers, attorneys, or vendors who want access to that market.

### Signal Copy
**The directory gives the message variable.**

Use when: the source has strong per-row context but the directory is not your full ICP. Pull permit type, award amount, licence class, certification status, event name, location, or regulator as the custom variable that makes the first line specific.

### Market Map
**The directory teaches the terrain.**

Use when: you are entering a niche and need vocabulary, segments, buyer types, associations, categories, and market structure before writing copy. The output may be a strategy brief first, then a list second.

## Offer-to-Directory Routing Playbooks

### Workbench Rule
Every useful playbook should answer five questions:
1. What does the directory tell you?
2. Who cares?
3. What problem is implied?
4. What offer angle follows?
5. What would the first line reference?

---

### Playbook 1: Revenue Advisory into Active Growth Markets

**Source Families:** Trade Show, Gov Procurement, Grant & Award, Tech Partner, Certification

- **Signal:** Exhibitor booth, contract win, partner listing, recent grant, or certification used for market positioning.
- **Buyer:** Founder, CEO, Head of Sales, Growth Lead, or partner-led services firm owner.
- **Implication:** The company is already investing in market access, but may not have a predictable outbound system around that market.
- **Offer Angle:** Turn the signal into a campaign wedge: more meetings from the market they are already trying to enter.
- **Example Opener:** "Saw you exhibited at [show] — usually that means you are trying to get in front of more [buyer type] this quarter."

---

### Playbook 2: Commercial Finance Around Live Activity

**Source Families:** Building Permit, Construction & CRE, Gov Procurement, Manufacturing, Grant & Award

- **Signal:** Permit filing, contract award, expansion incentive, supplier listing, or active project record.
- **Buyer:** Owner, CFO, COO, principal, developer, contractor, manufacturer, or procurement-heavy operator.
- **Implication:** Growth or project execution may be creating cash timing gaps, equipment needs, bonding constraints, or receivables pressure.
- **Offer Angle:** Position capital as execution support tied to a known project or revenue event, not generic financing.
- **Example Opener:** "Noticed the [city] permit tied to [project type] — reaching out because projects like that can create timing gaps before invoices catch up."

---

### Playbook 3: Ops, AI, and Automation for Complex Operators

**Source Families:** Manufacturing, Healthcare, Logistics, Agency/Software, Vendor Registry

- **Signal:** Multi-location operation, regulated provider listing, carrier record, partner ecosystem listing, or vendor registration.
- **Buyer:** COO, operations lead, founder, practice administrator, logistics manager, or service-business owner.
- **Implication:** The business likely has manual handoffs, compliance admin, scheduling load, reporting needs, or fragmented customer intake.
- **Offer Angle:** Tie automation to one operational bottleneck implied by the source, not a broad "AI can help" claim.
- **Example Opener:** "Found you through the [registry] — operators in that category usually have a lot of intake and admin work sitting between teams."

---

### Playbook 4: Compliance and Risk Services into Regulated Lists (Reverse Build)

**Source Families:** Environmental, Contractor Licence, Certification, Building Permit, Financial & Insurance

- **Signal:** Regulated facility, citation, permit, licence class, certification requirement, or industry-specific compliance registry.
- **Buyer:** Compliance consultants, environmental firms, insurance agencies, law firms, auditors, safety consultants.
- **Implication:** The listed companies likely have reporting burden, audit risk, insurance needs, remediation work, or safety requirements.
- **Offer Angle:** Bring the service provider a market map or appointment flow around companies with known compliance events.
- **Example Opener:** "Found a group of [state] facilities with recent [compliance signal]. Is that a market you are already trying to get in front of?"

---

### Playbook 5: Hiring, Staffing, and Workforce Pressure (Signal Copy)

**Source Families:** Workforce, Healthcare, Manufacturing, Construction & CRE, Business Licence

- **Signal:** New licence, facility type, trade category, provider registry, workforce grant, or expansion record.
- **Buyer:** Owner, HR lead, operations manager, administrator, plant manager, field-service leader.
- **Implication:** The business depends on scarce roles, shift coverage, credentialed labor, or skilled trades to fulfill demand.
- **Offer Angle:** Reference the operating category and connect staffing to throughput, service capacity, or open-market expansion.
- **Example Opener:** "Saw you are listed as a licensed [trade/provider type] in [market] — curious if labor capacity is limiting how much work you can take on."

---

### Playbook 6: Local and Regional B2B Services (Market Map)

**Source Families:** Business Licence, Chambers & Local, Contractor Licence, Building Permit, Company Registry

- **Signal:** City licence, local membership, new business registration, permit activity, or category concentration in one MSA.
- **Buyer:** Local operators, franchisees, contractors, clinics, agencies, trades, professional services, regional B2B firms.
- **Implication:** The company is active in a defined geography where competition, regulation, customer acquisition, and vendor relationships are local.
- **Offer Angle:** Make the outreach feel native to the market: local category, recent registration, nearby activity, or regional growth constraint.
- **Example Opener:** "Found you through [city]'s licence data while mapping [category] operators in the area."

## Discovery Playbook: Use Platform Footprints to Find More Directories

A footprint is not the directory itself. It is the software or ecosystem that hosts many directories. Search for the footprint plus your industry, geography, or buyer category to surface sources that are not already in this bank.

### What a Footprint Tells You
Footprints are useful only when the platform leaves an indexable trace on real directory pages. The best footprints are phrases that show up on member directories, chamber pages, open-data portals, procurement portals, and exhibitor tools.

**Strong footprints:** "Powered by Wild Apricot", "Powered By GrowthZone", "Socrata", "Bonfire Procurement Portal"
**Directory phrases:** "member directory", "active member directory", "business license", "supplier portal", "exhibitor list"

### Formula
`"footprint phrase" + "directory phrase" + "market term"`

### Four Steps
1. Start in Google.
2. Use the formula with quotes to keep exact phrases together.
3. Generate combinations — swap platform, directory phrase, market term.
4. Open results and validate: named companies, filters, profiles, locations, categories, dates, or signals.

**Reject Rule:** If the result does not expose companies, members, vendors, exhibitors, records, or profiles, it is not a sourcing asset.

### Footprint Platform Categories

**Association platforms:** Wild Apricot, MemberClicks, GrowthZone, ChamberMaster, Glue Up, YourMembership
- Pattern: `"Powered by Wild Apricot" "member directory" "[trade]"`

**Event platforms:** Map Your Show, ExpoFP, Swapcard, Brella, EventHub, 10times
- Pattern: `"exhibitor list" "[industry]" "Map Your Show"`

**Government data platforms:** Socrata, ArcGIS Hub, OpenDataSoft, CivicPlus, Accela Citizen Access, Tyler EnerGov
- Pattern: `"Socrata" "active businesses" "business license"`

**Procurement platforms:** B2Gnow, Bonfire, DemandStar, BidNet Direct, PlanetBids, MERX
- Pattern: `"Bonfire Procurement Portal" "vendor registration" "[category]"`

## The MSA Signal Method: Repeatable Market-by-Market List Building

Use when geography matters. Instead of scraping a national list and hoping the copy feels relevant, choose one market, find the public signals inside that market, and build a campaign around what those local companies are visibly doing.

### When to Use It
- **Use for:** Local B2B services, commercial finance, recruiting, contractor services, professional services, healthcare operations, franchise support, and regional demand generation.
- **Avoid for:** Offers where geography adds no buying context. If the problem is purely software-category, role-based, or enterprise-wide, use a source-family playbook instead.
- **Success test:** The first line should be able to reference a market-specific fact.

### Five-Step Build Sequence
1. **Choose the market** — Pick one MSA, county, state, or dense metro.
2. **Find the source** — Use the bank, footprint searches, or operators to locate public directories tied to that geography.
3. **Filter before pulling** — Narrow by licence class, industry, permit type, award category, certification, city, or status.
4. **Enrich selectively** — Clean the company set first, then enrich only the firms that match.
5. **Write from proof** — Reference the local signal and connect it to the problem the offer solves.

### Six MSA Signal Types

| Signal Type | What It Implies | Best Buyer | Example Opener |
|---|---|---|---|
| New business licence | New local activity, market entry, entity setup | Local operators, new service businesses, franchises | "Saw you recently got licensed in [city] — reaching out because new operators usually have early demand and vendor gaps." |
| Commercial building permit | Expansion, renovation, capex, timing pressure | Owners, developers, contractors, franchisees | "Noticed the [city] permit tied to [project type] — projects like that tend to create timing gaps." |
| Contractor or trade licence | Regulated trade with local service territory | Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, engineering | "Found you through [state]'s licensed [trade] registry while mapping operators around [city]." |
| Certified supplier | Procurement positioning, needs contract access | Certified contractors, professional services | "Saw your [certification] listing in [state] — curious whether you are actively pursuing more procurement work." |
| Award or grant recipient | Recent funding, execution needs, hiring | Government vendors, contractors, manufacturers | "Noticed [company] was awarded [contract/grant] in [state] — wins like that usually create execution needs." |
| Regulated facility | Compliance burden, reporting, insurance complexity | Industrial facilities, processors, manufacturers | "Found you in [regulator]'s facility data while looking at operators around [county]." |

## Navigating Complex Directories

### Core Operating Rule
Map the source before you pull the list.

### Six Steps
1. **Identify the signal** — What does being listed actually mean?
2. **Find the constraints** — Geography, date, status, trade class, industry filters.
3. **Inspect the fields** — Legal name, DBA, website, domain, phone, address, licence number, issue date, profile URL.
4. **Test a narrow pull** — Run one small segment first.
5. **Choose the extraction path** — CSV/API first, public HTML second, profile-page crawl third, manual sampling last.
6. **Preserve the context** — Keep source name, source URL, filter used, signal type, signal date, and profile URL beside every company row.

### Source Types

| Type | Best Move | Watch For |
|---|---|---|
| Search portals | Start with exact filters, then expand | CAPTCHAs, session URLs, hidden profile pages, result caps |
| Open-data exports | Download dataset, filter locally | Stale update dates, duplicate entities, inactive statuses |
| Profile directories | Use category pages and profile URLs | Infinite scroll, JS-rendered results, duplicate office locations |
| PDFs and reports | Extract tables first, inspect a sample | Line breaks in names, merged cells, scanned pages |

### Reusable Asset: Document the Directory Once
For sources you will use repeatedly, create a short operating brief:
- Source purpose
- Useful filters
- Extraction route
- Required columns
- Known failure modes
- Message angle

## 100-Point Source Scoring Model

| Points | Criterion | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Signal quality | The listing proves an action or state |
| 15 | Buyer fit | Companies match the offer, budget, geography, industry |
| 15 | Enrichment handles | Rows include legal name, domain, website, LinkedIn, address |
| 10 | Filterability | Can be narrowed before extraction |
| 10 | Timing relevance | Signal is recent enough to justify the email |
| 10 | Copy specificity | Gives you a concrete first-line reason |
| 10 | Extraction path | CSV/API is best; public HTML workable; profile crawls need care |
| 10 | Scale and reuse | Same source supports multiple markets, segments, dates |

### Score Ranges
- **80-100 (Scrape Now):** Strong signal, clean data, relevant buyers, realistic extraction
- **60-79 (Pilot First):** Pull a filtered sample before committing
- **40-59 (Use Selectively):** Market map or copy only, not a clean campaign list yet
- **Below 40 (Skip):** Signal weak, data stale, buyers wrong, or extraction not justified

### Kill Rules — Stop Before Scraping If:
- No named companies or entities are visible
- Source only lists consumers/individuals when offer needs companies
- You cannot explain why this listing creates a better reason to write
- Data is stale, undated, inactive, or from an old event cycle
- Rows have no usable enrichment handle beyond a vague trade name
- Extraction path is too fragile
- Resulting email would still sound like a generic industry blast

## Best Source Categories by Use Case

| Category | Best For | Use When | First Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent action signals (permits, awards, licences, exhibitors) | Timing | Offer solves a problem that appears after the event | Filter by date first |
| Old-economy universes (manufacturing, suppliers, contractors) | Coverage | Offer benefits from fragmented, under-contacted markets | Choose a subcategory |
| Contract-aware companies (SAM.gov, vendor portals, DBE/WBE) | Procurement | Campaign relates to financing, bonding, compliance, staffing | Separate eligibility from activity |
| MSA/regional builds (city licences, permits, chambers) | Local | Geography gives the email credibility | Pick one market, build depth |
| Partner ecosystems (AWS, Microsoft, Salesforce, HubSpot) | Channel | Offer helps agencies/MSPs/consultants | Filter by service line |
| Market-map sources (associations, broad registries) | Learning | Need to understand the niche before building | Use it to find sharper sources |

## After the Scrape: Enrichment with AI Ark

A directory gives you the company universe and the signal. AI Ark turns that into people, titles, verified emails, and account filters.

### Workflow
1. **Export the company list** — Keep company name, domain, LinkedIn URL, city/state, source name, signal.
2. **Match accounts first** — Use company name or domain in AI Ark.
3. **Filter for the right people** — Find current buyers by role.
4. **Export only usable contacts** — Pull verified emails + the original directory signal.

### Core Rule
Keep the source signal attached to every enriched contact. Company, contact, role, source, and signal all stay in the same row.

### AI Ark API
- Company match: `POST https://api.ai-ark.com/api/developer-portal/v1/companies`
- People search: `POST https://api.ai-ark.com/api/developer-portal/v1/people`
- Required headers: `X-TOKEN: YOUR_API_KEY` + `Content-Type: application/json`

## Final Execution Sequence

1. State the offer — Write the problem the campaign solves before touching the directory.
2. Name the signal — Identify what the source proves about the company.
3. Score the source — Use the 100-point model and apply the kill rules.
4. Choose the motion — Direct Source Build, Reverse Build, Signal Copy, or Market Map.
5. Run the smallest proof — Pull one segment, enrich it, write five openers, then scale.
